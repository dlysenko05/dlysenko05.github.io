NSInduct

Torsional magnetic winding and braking in a spherical shell.

How does a wound field brake the flow?

Differential rotation winds a poloidal magnetic field into a toroidal one. The resulting Maxwell stress exerts a torque on the flow and transfers energy out of differential rotation. I built NSInduct to isolate that feedback and test how the energy exchange changes with the initial field.

The model evolves axisymmetric torsional resistive magnetohydrodynamics in a constant-density spherical shell, with a prescribed confined poloidal field. It includes toroidal magnetic diffusion and optional viscosity. The two implementations use conservative finite differences and a Dedalus v3 spectral solver, respectively.

Magnetic braking in the shell

With feedback enabled, doubling the seed field approximately halves the time for the kinetic energy of differential rotation to fall by half. In the no-feedback control without viscosity, the field winds up while the kinetic energy stays constant.

The half-times support approximate inverse-field Alfvén scaling for these initial conditions and this shell. They measure energy transfer in a reduced model with fixed poloidal flux. Estimating a stellar spin-down time would require a model for the star and its external torque.

Benchmarks and reproducibility

I checked both implementations against analytical winding and resistive-decay benchmarks, tracked energy and angular momentum, and ran matched cases to compare the solvers. The early energy exchange agrees closely. Late ideal wall values, phase-mixed gradients and pointwise extrema near the poles remain sensitive to resolution.

The release contains environment specifications, benchmark tests, reproduction scripts and three notebooks covering kinematic winding, braking, and resistivity and convergence. The tagged README gives the commands to reproduce the calculations.
