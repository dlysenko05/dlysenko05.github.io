Neutron-star structure

How the equation of state determines the mass and radius of a cold, nonrotating neutron star.

  • Ioffe Institute
  • With Dr. Mikhail Gusakov
  • Oct. 2024–Apr. 2026

Equation of state and equilibrium

I derived the relativistic zero-temperature equation of state for an ideal neutron–proton–electron Fermi gas. I then implemented six numerical solvers in Python for the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations, which determine hydrostatic equilibrium for a spherical star in general relativity.

To compare the solvers, I investigated numerical integration, interpolation of tabulated equations of state and treatment of the stellar surface. Using the published BSk24 equation of state as a benchmark, I reproduced its reference maximum mass and corresponding radius. I obtained about 2.28 solar masses (Mmax ≈ 2.28 M⊙) and R = 11.08 km.

My independent NSInduct project uses a reduced magnetic shell model to study a different question.
