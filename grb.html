Gamma-ray burst light curves

Estimating burst duration and pulse structure from photon counts.

  • Ioffe Institute
  • With Dr. Dmitry Svinkin
  • Sept. 2026–present

What can we recover from the counts?

Duration helps classify gamma-ray bursts, and the timing and separation of pulses give clues to how the emission evolves. The underlying burst signal has to be inferred from discrete photon counts on top of a background. Small changes in the inferred emission before or after the main pulse can move the endpoints of a duration estimate.

I recovered and adapted a nonstationary random-Fourier-feature model from the legacy pyipn/nazgul code. I isolated the Stan workflow for a Poisson count likelihood and repaired numerical failures in the likelihood and feature-scale parametrization.

Synthetic and observed bursts

I tested the reconstruction on synthetic single-pulse, double-pulse and faint light curves. The errors, measured as root-mean-square error relative to the peak signal, were approximately 3%, 3.3% and 7.2%, respectively. I also used simulated light curves to test how well the method could recover an imposed time delay between detectors.

I applied the model to time-tagged events from three Fermi/GBM bursts and checked sensitivity to detector choice and time bins of 16–64 ms. For two short bursts, fixing the Fourier frequencies improved sampling. Nominal 90% posterior predictive intervals contained observed counts in 76–78% of the bins for those bursts.

The synthetic tests also showed overconfidence around some sharp features at high signal-to-noise. I’m using these checks to study when weak reconstructed emission affects duration estimates and how much fine structure the data support. Model-based durations depend on the analysis window and background treatment, so I distinguish them from the catalog T90.
