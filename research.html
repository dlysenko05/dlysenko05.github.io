01Ioffe Institute · 2026–present

Quasar 4C 04.81: absorption and accretion-disk structure

With Dr. Sergei Balashev

For the proximate absorber at z ≈ 2.58, I analyze high-resolution VLT/UVES spectra with Python and SPECTRO to examine how the absorbing gas covers the quasar's emitting region. My work includes multi-transition Fe II fitting, component-specific partial-covering models, and tests of disk–absorber geometry.

Fits to Fe II fine-structure transitions show excitation-dependent effective partial covering. In an 830-pixel joint full-profile test, the best completed component-specific model was favored over the best common-covering model by ΔBIC ≈ 122. Separately, continuum fitting gave a disk temperature-law exponent β = 0.571 ± 0.036, subject to continuum and reddening systematics.

The Fe II data do not yet establish an independent wavelength-dependent covering law or a disk-temperature exponent inferred from absorption. Those questions require additional line-profile, geometry, and identifiability tests.

02Ioffe Institute · 2026–present

Gamma-ray burst timing and morphology

With Dr. Dmitry Svinkin

I am rebuilding and testing a Bayesian random-Fourier-feature model for GRB light curves, using Poisson count likelihoods and Stan/CmdStanPy. The aim is to characterize burst duration and pulse structure while keeping track of background and model uncertainty.

On Fermi/GBM data for GRB 200826A, the pipeline recovers the dominant pulse across several detectors and 16–64 ms time bins. For the strongest detector, the model's source-only finite-window duration is around 0.9–1.0 seconds. Synthetic tests also demonstrated a prototype for inter-detector time-delay estimation.

The fitted duration is not interchangeable with the published catalog T90. A longer inferred tail in one detector and the reliability of fine-scale substructure remain under validation; this is not yet a multi-burst population result.

03Ioffe Institute · 2024–2026

Neutron-star structure and magnetic-field evolution

With Dr. Mikhail Gusakov

I implemented relativistic equations of state and six numerical solvers for the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations. With the BSk24 equation of state, the calculations recovered a maximum mass of 2.28 solar masses.

In related magnetic-field studies, I used kinematic winding calculations in spherical geometries and reduced models to explore toroidal-field growth, Ohmic diffusion, and the braking of differential rotation through torsional magnetic feedback.

The focused torsional resistive-MHD project is a reduced spatially resolved PDE study; it is not a full vector-MHD production simulation.

04ITMO University · 2026–present

Dielectric metasurfaces at microwave frequencies

With Dr. Alexander Solomonov

At the ITMO Metamaterials Laboratory I designed and simulated a 5 GHz, three-layer dielectric meta-atom and used the resulting phase library to construct a 377-element beam deflector and metalens. The workflow combines COMSOL, Python-driven Lumerical FDTD, and LightPipes.

For a 20° deflection target, full-wave and scalar models yielded 20.49° and 19.71°, respectively. The metalens models disagreed on the focal position, which remains an unresolved modeling question rather than a confirmed focusing result.
